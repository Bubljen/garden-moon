Put it in your phone's calendar

One event per day says what the day is good for, with the full details inside. Choose an option with reminders to get an alert at 8 pm the evening before.

iPhone & iPad: with reminders (needs a Mac)

Reminders can only be added from a computer. The iPhone can't import them on its own.

  1. On your Mac, download the calendar file.
    Download calendar file
  2. Open Calendar → File → New Calendar → iCloud, and name it "Sowing".
  3. File → Import, choose the downloaded file, and add it to Sowing.
  4. It syncs to your iPhone, with an 8 pm reminder the evening before each day.

This is a one-off copy, so come back next year for new dates.

iPhone & iPad: auto-updating, no reminders

  1. Tap Subscribe, then confirm.
    Subscribe

Each day's type always shows in your calendar and updates itself, but the iPhone doesn't keep reminders from a subscribed calendar.

Android: with reminders

  1. Install ICSx⁵ (Google Play or F-Droid). It adds online calendars to your phone's own calendar.
  2. In ICSx⁵ tap + and paste the calendar address below.
  3. Leave alerts on. The calendar then shows up in Google Calendar or your phone's calendar app, with the 8 pm reminders.

Android: without an extra app

  1. On a computer, open calendar.google.com.
  2. Next to Other calendars click + → From URL, paste the address below, and click Add calendar.
  3. On the phone, open Google Calendar → Settings → find Sowing calendar → turn on Sync.

Google doesn't allow reminders on subscribed calendars, so you'll see each day's type but get no 8 pm alert.

How to read it. The Moon's position in the star constellations sets the day type: Earth constellations favour roots, Water favours leaves, Air favours flowers, Fire favours fruit and seed. In the Southern Hemisphere the descending Moon (▼ planting time) is for transplanting, pruning, compost and soil work. The ascending Moon (▲) favours sowing, grafting and harvesting above-ground crops.

Grey marks are times to avoid sowing and planting: lunar nodes (6 h before to 3 h after), perigee (±12 h) and eclipses (±12 h). Times are rounded to the hour, as in Thun's printed calendar. Sow early in a window rather than at its edge. Constellation boundaries were checked against 19 published Thun-style transitions, all within an hour.
